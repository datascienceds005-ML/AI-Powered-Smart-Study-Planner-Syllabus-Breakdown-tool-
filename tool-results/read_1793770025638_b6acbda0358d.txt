    14→## 1. Introduction
    15→
    16→### 1.1 Purpose
    17→
    18→The AI-Powered Smart Study Planner & Syllabus Breakdown Tool is a
    19→student-focused application that converts a syllabus into a practical,
    20→personalized study schedule. Students provide their subjects or
    21→chapters, examination date, available daily study hours, and familiarity
    22→with each topic. The application estimates topic complexity, allocates
    23→preparation time, and generates a day-by-day study plan.
    24→
    25→This document defines the functional requirements, non-functional
    26→requirements, data model, algorithms, interfaces, constraints,
    27→assumptions, and acceptance criteria for the proposed system. It is
    28→intended for student developers, project guides, evaluators, testers,
    29→and future maintainers.
    30→
    31→### 1.2 Problem Statement
    32→
    33→Students preparing for semester examinations and competitive
    34→examinations often have difficulty deciding: - Which chapters should be
    35→studied first. - How much time each topic requires. - How to divide a
    36→large syllabus into daily targets. - How to adjust a plan when the
    37→examination date is near. - How to monitor completed work and identify
    38→topics that need revision.
    39→
    40→A fixed timetable does not account for differences in topic difficulty
    41→or a student's existing knowledge. The proposed application addresses
    42→this problem by estimating topic preparation effort and distributing
    43→available study time according to topic complexity, familiarity,
    44→priority, and the time remaining before the examination.
    45→
    46→### 1.3 Project Objectives
    47→
    48→The system shall:
    49→
    50→1.  Allow students to select a predefined subject or enter a custom
    51→    syllabus.
    52→2.  Accept chapter-level topic information and difficulty-related
    53→    attributes.
    54→3.  Calculate a transparent Topic Complexity Score.
    55→4.  Estimate preparation time for each topic.
    56→5.  Personalize estimates using the student's familiarity level.
    57→6.  Generate a realistic schedule between the current date and the
    58→    examination date.
    59→7.  Prioritize important topics when preparation time is limited.
    60→8.  Display the schedule through a calendar, timeline, or Gantt chart.
    61→9.  Allow students to record progress and mark topics as completed.
    62→10. Recalculate remaining targets when progress or available time
    63→    changes.
    64→
    65→### 1.4 Scope
    66→
    67→**Included in scope** - Subject and chapter selection. - Custom syllabus
    68→entry and optional CSV upload. - Topic difficulty and preparation-time
    69→estimation using a rule-based model. - Familiarity-based
    70→personalization. - Exam-date and daily-study-hours configuration. -
    71→Day-wise study schedule generation. - High-yield prioritization under
    72→time constraints. - Progress tracking and schedule summaries. -
    73→Interactive charts and downloadable schedule data. - Local file-based
    74→subject and topic dataset.
    75→
    76→**Outside the initial scope** - Automatic extraction of a complete
    77→syllabus from arbitrary PDFs or images. - Guaranteed prediction of
    78→examination questions. - Automatic access to university or
    79→examination-board portals. - Live collaboration between multiple
    80→students. - Paid tutoring, video lessons, or learning-management
    81→functionality. - A trained machine-learning model requiring a large
    82→labeled dataset. - Mobile-native applications and offline
    83→synchronization across devices.
    84→
    85→These features may be considered for future versions.

   140→## 2. Overall Description
   141→
   142→### 2.1 Product Perspective
   143→
   144→The application is a standalone, data-driven Python web application.
   145→Streamlit provides the user interface, while a separate planning engine
   146→validates inputs, calculates complexity and estimated effort, and
   147→generates the schedule. pandas and NumPy support tabular processing and
   148→calculations. Plotly or Altair presents the schedule and progress
   149→visually.
   150→
   151→The system can run locally on a standard laptop. It does not require a
   152→GPU, a dedicated server, or a cloud database for the initial version.
   153→

   303→
   304→**FR-07 --- Examination Date**\

   309→

   311→The system shall allow the student to select a familiarity level for

   326→

   328→
   329→**FR-12 --- Complexity Score**\
   330→The system shall calculate a complexity score for each topic using a
   331→weighted formula based on mathematical density, conceptual depth, and
   332→historical difficulty.
   333→
   334→**FR-13 --- Familiarity Adjustment**\
   335→The system shall adjust the estimated preparation time according to the
   336→student's familiarity rating.
   337→
   338→**FR-14 --- Preparation-Time Estimation**\
   339→The system shall estimate the hours required for every topic using its
   340→baseline preparation time and complexity/familiarity adjustments.
   341→
   342→**FR-15 --- Topic Priority**\
   343→The system shall calculate a priority value using available information
   344→such as topic importance, complexity, and preparation effort.
   345→
   346→**FR-16 --- Transparent Results**\
   347→The system shall show the complexity score, familiarity adjustment, and
   348→estimated preparation time so that students can understand why a topic
   349→received its allocation.
   350→
   351→### 3.4 Study Schedule Generation
   352→
   353→**FR-17 --- Available-Time Calculation**\
   354→The system shall calculate the number of available study days between
   355→the current date and the examination date.
   356→
   357→**FR-18 --- Daily Capacity**\
   358→The system shall calculate daily study capacity using the student's
   359→available hours and any configured rest days or daily limits.
   360→
   361→**FR-19 --- Day-by-Day Plan**\
   362→The system shall distribute topics across available days and generate a
   363→dated daily study schedule.
   364→
   365→**FR-20 --- Workload Balancing**\
   366→The system should distribute study hours as evenly as practical while
   367→respecting topic effort estimates and daily capacity.
   368→
   369→**FR-21 --- Topic Ordering**\
   370→The system shall consider topic priority and prerequisite order, if
   371→prerequisite information is supplied.
   372→
   373→**FR-22 --- High-Yield Mode**\
   374→When total estimated preparation time exceeds available study time, the
   375→system shall activate a constrained planning mode. It shall prioritize

   378→
   379→**FR-23 --- Revision Buffer**\

   382→
   383→**FR-24 --- Insufficient-Time Warning**\
   384→The system shall warn the student when the available time is
   385→insufficient to complete all topics at their estimated preparation
   386→times.
   387→
   388→**FR-25 --- Schedule Regeneration**\
   389→The system shall regenerate the remaining schedule when the student
   390→changes the exam date, daily study hours, topic list, or other relevant
   391→planning inputs.

   564→## 5. Complexity and Planning Algorithms
   565→
   566→### 5.1 Complexity Score
   567→
   568→The complexity engine shall use a weighted, explainable rule-based
   569→formula.
   570→
   571→Let:
   572→
   573→-   (M_i) = Mathematical Density of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   574→-   (C_i) = Conceptual Depth of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   575→-   (H_i) = Historical Difficulty of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   576→-   (w_1, w_2, w_3) = configurable weights.
   577→
   578→The raw complexity score is:
   579→
   580→\[ RawScore_i = (w_1 `\times `{=tex}M_i) + (w_2 `\times `{=tex}C_i) +
   581→(w_3 `\times `{=tex}H_i) \]
   582→
   583→The weights shall sum to 1.0. A suggested initial configuration is:
   584→
   585→\[ w_1 = 0.30,`\quad `{=tex}w_2 = 0.40,`\quad `{=tex}w_3 = 0.30 \]
   586→
   587→The normalized complexity score on a 0--100 scale is:
   588→
   589→\[ Complexity_i = `\frac{RawScore_i - 1}{4}`{=tex} `\times 100`{=tex} \]
   590→
   591→This normalization assumes all three input ratings use a 1--5 scale and
   592→the weights sum to 1.0.
   593→
   594→Suggested interpretation:
   595→
   596→  Score     Difficulty category
   597→  --------- ---------------------
   598→  0--33     Low
   599→  34--66    Medium
   600→  67--100   High
   601→
   602→The exact category boundaries should be configurable.
   603→
   604→### 5.2 Familiarity Adjustment
   605→
   606→The student's familiarity level modifies the baseline preparation time.
   607→
   608→Suggested initial multipliers:
   609→
   610→  Familiarity             Multiplier
   611→  --------------------- ------------
   612→  Completely New                1.50
   613→  Basic Understanding           1.20
   614→  Familiar                      1.00
   615→  Revised Before                0.75
   616→
   617→These values are initial project assumptions and should be tested with
   618→sample student feedback.
   619→
   620→### 5.3 Preparation-Time Estimation
   621→
   622→If a baseline preparation time exists, the system shall calculate:
   623→
   624→\[ EstimatedHours_i = BaseHours_i `\times `{=tex}ComplexityFactor_i
   625→`\times `{=tex}FamiliarityMultiplier_i \]
   626→
   627→A suggested complexity factor is:
   628→
   629→\[ ComplexityFactor_i = 0.75 + `\frac{Complexity_i}{200}`{=tex} \]
   630→
   631→This produces a factor from 0.75 to 1.25 when the normalized score
   632→ranges from 0 to 100.
   633→
   634→If baseline hours are unavailable, the application may estimate them
   635→from page count and a configurable base reading/study rate. The chosen
   636→fallback rule must be visible to the student and documented in the
   637→application.
   638→
   639→The system shall round displayed estimates to a practical precision,
   640→such as the nearest 0.25 hour, while retaining sufficient precision
   641→internally for scheduling.
   642→
   643→### 5.4 Topic Priority
   644→
   645→A suggested priority score is:
   646→
   647→\[ Priority_i = (0.50 `\times `{=tex}Importance_i) + (0.30
   648→`\times `{=tex}NormalizedComplexity_i) + (0.20
   649→`\times `{=tex}NormalizedEffort_i) \]
   650→
   651→All three inputs must first be normalized to a common 0--100 scale. The
   652→weights are configurable. The application should explain that priority
   653→is a planning aid, not a prediction of examination questions.
   654→
   655→If syllabus importance is unavailable, the system shall use a documented
   656→fallback and shall not silently treat missing importance as verified
   657→exam weightage.
   658→
   659→### 5.5 Available-Time Calculation
   660→
   661→Let:
   662→
   663→-   \(D\) = number of usable study days before the examination.
   664→-   (H_d) = available study hours per day.
   665→-   \(B\) = hours reserved for revision or practice, if enabled.
   666→
   667→Then:
   668→
   669→\[ AvailableHours = (D `\times `{=tex}H_d) - B \]
   670→
   671→The examination day shall not be counted as a full study day by default.
   672→The system shall prevent schedules from assigning work after the
   673→examination date.
   674→
   675→### 5.6 Schedule Generation
   676→
   677→The schedule generator shall:
   678→
   679→1.  Validate the exam date and daily study availability.
   680→2.  Determine usable study days and rest days.
   681→3.  Calculate estimated hours and priority for every topic.
   682→4.  Respect prerequisite order when prerequisite information is
   683→    provided.
   684→5.  Sort or select topics using priority and prerequisite constraints.
   685→6.  Allocate topic hours across available days without exceeding daily
   686→    capacity.
   687→7.  Split long topics across multiple days when necessary.
   688→8.  Reserve revision time if configured.
   689→9.  identify any unallocated or unscheduled work.
   690→10. Return a schedule with dates, topics, hours, and status.
   691→
   692→The implementation may use a greedy allocation algorithm for the first
   693→version. The algorithm should be deterministic for the same inputs so
   694→that results are repeatable and testable.
   695→
   696→### 5.7 High-Yield Mode
   697→
   698→High-Yield Mode shall activate when:
   699→
   700→\[ TotalEstimatedHours \> AvailableHours \]
   701→
   702→In this mode, the application shall: - Prefer topics with higher
   703→priority. - Respect known prerequisites where possible. - Allocate
   704→available time according to priority and estimated effort. - Mark topics
   705→receiving reduced time. - Identify topics that could not be scheduled. -
   706→Show a clear warning that the plan is compressed and may not cover every
   707→topic adequately.
   708→
   709→The system shall never imply that a compressed plan guarantees
   710→examination readiness.
   711→
   712→### 5.8 Schedule Quality Rules
   713→
   714→The generated plan should satisfy these rules:
   715→
   716→-   No task shall have a negative duration.
   717→-   No daily allocation shall exceed the configured daily limit.
   718→-   No task shall be scheduled after the exam date.
   719→-   Every input topic shall be accounted for as scheduled, completed,
   720→    deferred, or unscheduled.
   721→-   Total assigned hours shall not exceed available hours.
   722→-   Prerequisites should be scheduled before dependent topics when
   723→    feasible.
   724→-   Revision time shall not overlap with the examination date.
   725→-   Rounding shall not cause the daily capacity to be exceeded.

   804→## 7. Non-Functional Requirements
   805→
   806→### 7.1 Performance
   807→
   808→**NFR-01:** For a typical syllabus containing up to 200 topics, schedule
   809→generation should normally complete within 3 seconds on a standard
   810→student laptop, excluding unusually slow file operations.
   811→
   812→**NFR-02:** Dashboard interactions should respond without unnecessary
   813→full-page delays.
   814→
   815→**NFR-03:** The application should avoid unnecessary large datasets and
   816→heavy model dependencies.
   817→
   818→### 7.2 Usability
   819→
   820→**NFR-04:** A first-time user should be able to create a basic study
   821→plan without technical assistance.
   822→
   823→**NFR-05:** Error messages shall explain the problem and suggest
   824→corrective action.
   825→
   826→**NFR-06:** Complexity and priority scores shall be accompanied by brief
   827→explanations.
   828→
   829→**NFR-07:** Charts shall include readable labels, legends, and tooltips
   830→where supported.
   831→
   832→### 7.3 Reliability
   833→
   834→**NFR-08:** The system shall validate input data before running
   835→calculations.
   836→
   837→**NFR-09:** Invalid or incomplete records shall not silently produce
   838→misleading schedules.
   839→
   840→**NFR-10:** The same valid inputs and configuration shall produce the
   841→same schedule.
   842→
   843→**NFR-11:** The application shall handle empty datasets, duplicate topic
   844→IDs, and missing optional fields safely.
   845→
   846→### 7.4 Maintainability
   847→
   848→**NFR-12:** UI code, data handling, complexity calculations, and
   849→scheduling logic should be separated into modules.
   850→
   851→**NFR-13:** Weights, multipliers, and thresholds shall be stored as
   852→configuration values rather than scattered throughout the codebase.
   853→
   854→**NFR-14:** The subject dataset shall be editable without changing the
   855→core scheduling algorithm.
   856→
   857→**NFR-15:** Functions responsible for calculations shall be
   858→independently testable.
   859→
   860→### 7.5 Portability
   861→
   862→**NFR-16:** The application shall run on supported Windows, Linux, and
   863→macOS environments with the required Python packages installed.
   864→
   865→**NFR-17:** The initial application shall not require GPU hardware.
   866→
   867→### 7.6 Security and Privacy
   868→
   869→**NFR-18:** The initial version shall not request unnecessary personal
   870→information.
   871→
   872→**NFR-19:** Uploaded CSV files shall be validated before processing.
   873→
   874→**NFR-20:** The application shall not execute uploaded file contents as
   875→code.
   876→
   877→**NFR-21:** If plans are stored locally, the application shall explain
   878→where the data is stored and how it can be reset.
   879→
   880→**NFR-22:** If deployed online in a future version, authentication,
   881→access control, secure storage, and transport security shall be reviewed
   882→before storing personal student data.
   883→
   884→### 7.7 Accessibility
   885→
   886→**NFR-23:** Important information shall not be communicated by color
   887→alone.
   888→
   889→**NFR-24:** Labels and chart text shall use readable contrast and sizes.
   890→
   891→**NFR-25:** Controls shall have clear labels and understandable
   892→instructions.

   896→## 8. Error Handling and Validation
   897→
   898→The application shall handle at least the following cases:
   899→
   900→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   901→  Condition                           Expected system response
   902→  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
   903→  Exam date is today or in the past   Show a validation error or request
   904→                                      a valid future date
   905→
   906→  Daily hours are zero or negative    Reject the value
   907→
   908→  Daily hours exceed configured       Ask the student to confirm or
   909→  maximum                             correct the value
   910→
   911→  No topics entered                   Do not generate a schedule; request
   912→                                      syllabus input
   913→
   914→  Duplicate topic IDs                 Reject or safely regenerate unique
   915→                                      IDs
   916→
   917→  Missing required CSV column         Display the missing column names
   918→
   919→  Invalid rating outside 1--5         Reject the affected record and
   920→                                      explain the allowed range
   921→
   922→  Negative page count or base hours   Reject the invalid value
   923→
   924→  Missing optional importance         Apply a documented fallback and
   925→                                      identify it
   926→
   927→  Estimated hours exceed capacity     Activate High-Yield Mode and show a
   928→                                      warning
   929→
   930→  No usable study days remain         Explain that a valid schedule
   931→                                      cannot be generated
   932→
   933→  Corrupt JSON/CSV file               Show a readable import error
   934→                                      without crashing
   935→
   936→  Progress file unavailable           Continue safely where possible and
   937→                                      explain that progress could not be
   938→                                      loaded or saved

  1060→## 12. Constraints and Limitations
  1061→
  1062→1.  Topic complexity is estimated from manually assigned attributes and
  1063→    is not an objective measurement of a student's ability.
  1064→2.  Familiarity is self-reported and may be inaccurate.
  1065→3.  Historical difficulty ratings may be approximate unless supported by
  1066→    reliable student-performance data.
  1067→4.  Preparation-time estimates are guidance values and may differ from
  1068→    actual study time.
  1069→5.  The initial version uses a rule-based model. It should not be
  1070→    described as a trained machine-learning model unless training data,
  1071→    model evaluation, and a genuine training process are implemented.
  1072→6.  High-Yield Mode helps prioritize limited time but cannot guarantee
  1073→    complete syllabus coverage or examination success.
  1074→7.  Automatic syllabus extraction and external syllabus synchronization
  1075→    are not part of the initial release.
  1076→8.  The quality of the schedule depends on the quality of the supplied
  1077→    topic data and student inputs.
