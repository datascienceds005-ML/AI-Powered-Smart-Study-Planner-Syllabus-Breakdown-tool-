    14→    14→## 1. Introduction
    15→    15→
    16→    16→### 1.1 Purpose
    17→    17→
    18→    18→The AI-Powered Smart Study Planner & Syllabus Breakdown Tool is a
    19→    19→student-focused application that converts a syllabus into a practical,
    20→    20→personalized study schedule. Students provide their subjects or
    21→    21→chapters, examination date, available daily study hours, and familiarity
    22→    22→with each topic. The application estimates topic complexity, allocates
    23→    23→preparation time, and generates a day-by-day study plan.
    24→    24→
    25→    25→This document defines the functional requirements, non-functional
    26→    26→requirements, data model, algorithms, interfaces, constraints,
    27→    27→assumptions, and acceptance criteria for the proposed system. It is
    28→    28→intended for student developers, project guides, evaluators, testers,
    29→    29→and future maintainers.
    30→    30→
    31→    31→### 1.2 Problem Statement
    32→    32→
    33→    33→Students preparing for semester examinations and competitive
    34→    34→examinations often have difficulty deciding: - Which chapters should be
    35→    35→studied first. - How much time each topic requires. - How to divide a
    36→    36→large syllabus into daily targets. - How to adjust a plan when the
    37→    37→examination date is near. - How to monitor completed work and identify
    38→    38→topics that need revision.
    39→    39→
    40→    40→A fixed timetable does not account for differences in topic difficulty
    41→    41→or a student's existing knowledge. The proposed application addresses
    42→    42→this problem by estimating topic preparation effort and distributing
    43→    43→available study time according to topic complexity, familiarity,
    44→    44→priority, and the time remaining before the examination.
    45→    45→
    46→    46→### 1.3 Project Objectives
    47→    47→
    48→    48→The system shall:
    49→    49→
    50→    50→1.  Allow students to select a predefined subject or enter a custom
    51→    51→    syllabus.
    52→    52→2.  Accept chapter-level topic information and difficulty-related
    53→    53→    attributes.
    54→    54→3.  Calculate a transparent Topic Complexity Score.
    55→    55→4.  Estimate preparation time for each topic.
    56→    56→5.  Personalize estimates using the student's familiarity level.
    57→    57→6.  Generate a realistic schedule between the current date and the
    58→    58→    examination date.
    59→    59→7.  Prioritize important topics when preparation time is limited.
    60→    60→8.  Display the schedule through a calendar, timeline, or Gantt chart.
    61→    61→9.  Allow students to record progress and mark topics as completed.
    62→    62→10. Recalculate remaining targets when progress or available time
    63→    63→    changes.
    64→    64→
    65→    65→### 1.4 Scope
    66→    66→
    67→    67→**Included in scope** - Subject and chapter selection. - Custom syllabus
    68→    68→entry and optional CSV upload. - Topic difficulty and preparation-time
    69→    69→estimation using a rule-based model. - Familiarity-based
    70→    70→personalization. - Exam-date and daily-study-hours configuration. -
    71→    71→Day-wise study schedule generation. - High-yield prioritization under
    72→    72→time constraints. - Progress tracking and schedule summaries. -
    73→    73→Interactive charts and downloadable schedule data. - Local file-based
    74→    74→subject and topic dataset.
    75→    75→
    76→    76→**Outside the initial scope** - Automatic extraction of a complete
    77→    77→syllabus from arbitrary PDFs or images. - Guaranteed prediction of
    78→    78→examination questions. - Automatic access to university or
    79→    79→examination-board portals. - Live collaboration between multiple
    80→    80→students. - Paid tutoring, video lessons, or learning-management
    81→    81→functionality. - A trained machine-learning model requiring a large
    82→    82→labeled dataset. - Mobile-native applications and offline
    83→    83→synchronization across devices.
    84→    84→
    85→    85→These features may be considered for future versions.

   140→   140→## 2. Overall Description
   141→   141→
   142→   142→### 2.1 Product Perspective
   143→   143→
   144→   144→The application is a standalone, data-driven Python web application.
   145→   145→Streamlit provides the user interface, while a separate planning engine
   146→   146→validates inputs, calculates complexity and estimated effort, and
   147→   147→generates the schedule. pandas and NumPy support tabular processing and
   148→   148→calculations. Plotly or Altair presents the schedule and progress
   149→   149→visually.
   150→   150→
   151→   151→The system can run locally on a standard laptop. It does not require a
   152→   152→GPU, a dedicated server, or a cloud database for the initial version.
   153→   153→

   266→   266→## 3. Functional Requirements
   267→   267→
   268→   268→Requirements are identified using IDs so that they can be referenced
   269→   269→during development and testing.
   270→   270→
   271→   271→### 3.1 Subject and Syllabus Management
   272→   272→
   273→   273→**FR-01 --- Subject Selection**\
   274→   274→The system shall display a list of predefined subjects from the internal
   275→   275→dataset.
   276→   276→
   277→   277→**FR-02 --- Subject Details**\
   278→   278→After subject selection, the system shall display the available modules,
   279→   279→chapters, or topics belonging to that subject.
   280→   280→
   281→   281→**FR-03 --- Custom Syllabus**\
   282→   282→The system shall allow a student to enter a custom subject name and add
   283→   283→chapters or topics manually.
   284→   284→
   285→   285→**FR-04 --- CSV Import**\
   286→   286→The system should allow the student to upload a CSV file containing
   287→   287→topic details. The system shall validate the file's columns and values
   288→   288→before accepting it.
   289→   289→
   290→   290→**FR-05 --- Topic Editing**\
   291→   291→The system shall allow the student to add, edit, and remove topics
   292→   292→before generating the schedule.
   293→   293→
   294→   294→**FR-06 --- Topic Attributes**\
   295→   295→Each topic shall support the following attributes: - Topic or chapter
   296→   296→name. - Estimated page count or content-length value. - Mathematical
   297→   297→density from 1 to 5. - Conceptual depth from 1 to 5. - Historical
   298→   298→difficulty or failure rating from 1 to 5. - Syllabus weightage or
   299→   299→importance, where known. - Baseline preparation time in hours, where
   300→   300→available.
   301→   301→
   302→   302→### 3.2 Student Input
   303→   303→
   304→   304→**FR-07 --- Examination Date**\
   305→   305→The system shall allow the student to select an examination date.
   306→   306→
   307→   307→**FR-08 --- Daily Study Availability**\
   308→   308→The system shall accept the number of study hours available per day.
   309→   309→
   310→   310→**FR-09 --- Familiarity Rating**\
   311→   311→The system shall allow the student to select a familiarity level for
   312→   312→every topic.
   313→   313→
   314→   314→The initial familiarity levels shall be: - Completely New. - Basic
   315→   315→Understanding. - Familiar. - Revised Before.
   316→   316→
   317→   317→**FR-10 --- Optional Study Preferences**\
   318→   318→The system may allow the student to specify: - Preferred study days. -
   319→   319→Weekly rest days. - Daily maximum study hours. - A revision buffer
   320→   320→before the examination. - A preferred daily study-session duration.
   321→   321→
   322→   322→**FR-11 --- Input Validation**\
   323→   323→The system shall reject invalid dates, negative study hours, empty topic
   324→   324→names, invalid rating values, and malformed uploaded files. It shall
   325→   325→show a clear message explaining how to correct the input.
   326→   326→
   327→   327→### 3.3 Complexity Analytics Engine
   328→   328→
   329→   329→**FR-12 --- Complexity Score**\
   330→   330→The system shall calculate a complexity score for each topic using a
   331→   331→weighted formula based on mathematical density, conceptual depth, and
   332→   332→historical difficulty.
   333→   333→
   334→   334→**FR-13 --- Familiarity Adjustment**\
   335→   335→The system shall adjust the estimated preparation time according to the
   336→   336→student's familiarity rating.
   337→   337→
   338→   338→**FR-14 --- Preparation-Time Estimation**\
   339→   339→The system shall estimate the hours required for every topic using its
   340→   340→baseline preparation time and complexity/familiarity adjustments.
   341→   341→
   342→   342→**FR-15 --- Topic Priority**\
   343→   343→The system shall calculate a priority value using available information
   344→   344→such as topic importance, complexity, and preparation effort.
   345→   345→
   346→   346→**FR-16 --- Transparent Results**\
   347→   347→The system shall show the complexity score, familiarity adjustment, and
   348→   348→estimated preparation time so that students can understand why a topic
   349→   349→received its allocation.
   350→   350→
   351→   351→### 3.4 Study Schedule Generation
   352→   352→
   353→   353→**FR-17 --- Available-Time Calculation**\
   354→   354→The system shall calculate the number of available study days between
   355→   355→the current date and the examination date.
   356→   356→
   357→   357→**FR-18 --- Daily Capacity**\
   358→   358→The system shall calculate daily study capacity using the student's
   359→   359→available hours and any configured rest days or daily limits.
   360→   360→
   361→   361→**FR-19 --- Day-by-Day Plan**\
   362→   362→The system shall distribute topics across available days and generate a
   363→   363→dated daily study schedule.
   364→   364→
   365→   365→**FR-20 --- Workload Balancing**\
   366→   366→The system should distribute study hours as evenly as practical while
   367→   367→respecting topic effort estimates and daily capacity.
   368→   368→
   369→   369→**FR-21 --- Topic Ordering**\
   370→   370→The system shall consider topic priority and prerequisite order, if
   371→   371→prerequisite information is supplied.
   372→   372→
   373→   373→**FR-22 --- High-Yield Mode**\
   374→   374→When total estimated preparation time exceeds available study time, the
   375→   375→system shall activate a constrained planning mode. It shall prioritize
   376→   376→high-importance topics and clearly identify topics that are shortened,
   377→   377→deferred, or not scheduled.
   378→   378→
   379→   379→**FR-23 --- Revision Buffer**\
   380→   380→If the student enables a revision buffer, the system should reserve the
   381→   381→configured time before the examination for revision and practice.
   382→   382→
   383→   383→**FR-24 --- Insufficient-Time Warning**\
   384→   384→The system shall warn the student when the available time is
   385→   385→insufficient to complete all topics at their estimated preparation
   386→   386→times.
   387→   387→
   388→   388→**FR-25 --- Schedule Regeneration**\
   389→   389→The system shall regenerate the remaining schedule when the student
   390→   390→changes the exam date, daily study hours, topic list, or other relevant
   391→   391→planning inputs.
   392→   392→
   393→   393→### 3.5 Dashboard and Visualization
   394→   394→
   395→   395→**FR-26 --- Dashboard Summary**\
   396→   396→The system shall display: - Subject name. - Examination date. - Days
   397→   397→remaining. - Total estimated study hours. - Available study hours. -
   398→   398→Number of topics. - Number of scheduled and completed topics.
   399→   399→
   400→   400→**FR-27 --- Daily Timeline**\
   401→   401→The system shall display the date, assigned topics, and estimated hours
   402→   402→for each study day.
   403→   403→
   404→   404→**FR-28 --- Gantt Chart**\
   405→   405→The system shall display an interactive timeline or Gantt-style chart
   406→   406→showing topic start dates, end dates, and duration.
   407→   407→
   408→   408→**FR-29 --- Priority Visualization**\
   409→   409→The system should visually distinguish high-, medium-, and low-priority
   410→   410→topics.
   411→   411→
   412→   412→**FR-30 --- Progress Visualization**\
   413→   413→The system shall display progress using a progress bar, chart, or
   414→   414→completion summary.
   415→   415→
   416→   416→**FR-31 --- Responsive Layout**\
   417→   417→The dashboard should remain usable on common laptop and desktop screen
   418→   418→sizes.
   419→   419→
   420→   420→### 3.6 Progress Tracking and Export
   421→   421→
   422→   422→**FR-32 --- Mark Completion**\
   423→   423→The system shall allow the student to mark a topic or scheduled task as
   424→   424→completed.
   425→   425→
   426→   426→**FR-33 --- Progress Update**\
   427→   427→The system shall update completion statistics when a task is marked
   428→   428→complete or incomplete.
   429→   429→
   430→   430→**FR-34 --- Progress Persistence**\
   431→   431→The system should preserve progress between application sessions using a
   432→   432→local data file or another configured persistence mechanism.
   433→   433→
   434→   434→**FR-35 --- Download Schedule**\
   435→   435→The system shall allow the student to download the generated schedule in
   436→   436→CSV format.
   437→   437→
   438→   438→**FR-36 --- Reset Plan**\
   439→   439→The system shall provide a clearly labeled option to reset the current
   440→   440→plan and its progress after confirmation.

   552→   552→  daily_hours         Available study hours per day
   553→   553→  topic_familiarity   Familiarity value for each topic
   554→   554→  scheduled_tasks     Topic/date/hour assignments
   555→   555→  completion_status   Pending, in progress, or completed
   556→   556→  created_at          Plan creation date/time
   557→   557→  updated_at          Last modification date/time
   558→   558→

   560→   560→not mandatory for the first release.

   563→   563→
   564→   564→## 5. Complexity and Planning Algorithms
   565→   565→
   566→   566→### 5.1 Complexity Score
   567→   567→
   568→   568→The complexity engine shall use a weighted, explainable rule-based
   569→   569→formula.
   570→   570→
   571→   571→Let:
   572→   572→
   573→   573→-   (M_i) = Mathematical Density of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   574→   574→-   (C_i) = Conceptual Depth of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   575→   575→-   (H_i) = Historical Difficulty of topic (i), from 1 to 5.
   576→   576→-   (w_1, w_2, w_3) = configurable weights.
   577→   577→
   578→   578→The raw complexity score is:
   579→   579→
   580→   580→\[ RawScore_i = (w_1 `\times `{=tex}M_i) + (w_2 `\times `{=tex}C_i) +
   581→   581→(w_3 `\times `{=tex}H_i) \]
   582→   582→
   583→   583→The weights shall sum to 1.0. A suggested initial configuration is:
   584→   584→
   585→   585→\[ w_1 = 0.30,`\quad `{=tex}w_2 = 0.40,`\quad `{=tex}w_3 = 0.30 \]
   586→   586→
   587→   587→The normalized complexity score on a 0--100 scale is:
   588→   588→
   589→   589→\[ Complexity_i = `\frac{RawScore_i - 1}{4}`{=tex} `\times 100`{=tex} \]
   590→   590→
   591→   591→This normalization assumes all three input ratings use a 1--5 scale and
   592→   592→the weights sum to 1.0.
   593→   593→
   594→   594→Suggested interpretation:
   595→   595→
   596→   596→  Score     Difficulty category
   597→   597→  --------- ---------------------
   598→   598→  0--33     Low
   599→   599→  34--66    Medium
   600→   600→  67--100   High
   601→   601→
   602→   602→The exact category boundaries should be configurable.
   603→   603→
   604→   604→### 5.2 Familiarity Adjustment
   605→   605→
   606→   606→The student's familiarity level modifies the baseline preparation time.
   607→   607→
   608→   608→Suggested initial multipliers:
   609→   609→
   610→   610→  Familiarity             Multiplier
   611→   611→  --------------------- ------------
   612→   612→  Completely New                1.50
   613→   613→  Basic Understanding           1.20
   614→   614→  Familiar                      1.00
   615→   615→  Revised Before                0.75
   616→   616→
   617→   617→These values are initial project assumptions and should be tested with
   618→   618→sample student feedback.
   619→   619→
   620→   620→### 5.3 Preparation-Time Estimation
   621→   621→
   622→   622→If a baseline preparation time exists, the system shall calculate:
   623→   623→
   624→   624→\[ EstimatedHours_i = BaseHours_i `\times `{=tex}ComplexityFactor_i
   625→   625→`\times `{=tex}FamiliarityMultiplier_i \]
   626→   626→
   627→   627→A suggested complexity factor is:
   628→   628→
   629→   629→\[ ComplexityFactor_i = 0.75 + `\frac{Complexity_i}{200}`{=tex} \]
   630→   630→
   631→   631→This produces a factor from 0.75 to 1.25 when the normalized score
   632→   632→ranges from 0 to 100.
   633→   633→
   634→   634→If baseline hours are unavailable, the application may estimate them
   635→   635→from page count and a configurable base reading/study rate. The chosen
   636→   636→fallback rule must be visible to the student and documented in the
   637→   637→application.
   638→   638→
   639→   639→The system shall round displayed estimates to a practical precision,
   640→   640→such as the nearest 0.25 hour, while retaining sufficient precision
   641→   641→internally for scheduling.
   642→   642→
   643→   643→### 5.4 Topic Priority
   644→   644→
   645→   645→A suggested priority score is:
   646→   646→
   647→   647→\[ Priority_i = (0.50 `\times `{=tex}Importance_i) + (0.30
   648→   648→`\times `{=tex}NormalizedComplexity_i) + (0.20
   649→   649→`\times `{=tex}NormalizedEffort_i) \]
   650→   650→
   651→   651→All three inputs must first be normalized to a common 0--100 scale. The
   652→   652→weights are configurable. The application should explain that priority
   653→   653→is a planning aid, not a prediction of examination questions.
   654→   654→
   655→   655→If syllabus importance is unavailable, the system shall use a documented
   656→   656→fallback and shall not silently treat missing importance as verified
   657→   657→exam weightage.
   658→   658→
   659→   659→### 5.5 Available-Time Calculation
   660→   660→
   661→   661→Let:
   662→   662→
   663→   663→-   \(D\) = number of usable study days before the examination.
   664→   664→-   (H_d) = available study hours per day.
   665→   665→-   \(B\) = hours reserved for revision or practice, if enabled.
   666→   666→
   667→   667→Then:
   668→   668→
   669→   669→\[ AvailableHours = (D `\times `{=tex}H_d) - B \]
   670→   670→
   671→   671→The examination day shall not be counted as a full study day by default.
   672→   672→The system shall prevent schedules from assigning work after the
   673→   673→examination date.
   674→   674→
   675→   675→### 5.6 Schedule Generation
   676→   676→
   677→   677→The schedule generator shall:
   678→   678→
   679→   679→1.  Validate the exam date and daily study availability.
   680→   680→2.  Determine usable study days and rest days.
   681→   681→3.  Calculate estimated hours and priority for every topic.
   682→   682→4.  Respect prerequisite order when prerequisite information is
   683→   683→    provided.
   684→   684→5.  Sort or select topics using priority and prerequisite constraints.
   685→   685→6.  Allocate topic hours across available days without exceeding daily
   686→   686→    capacity.
   687→   687→7.  Split long topics across multiple days when necessary.
   688→   688→8.  Reserve revision time if configured.
   689→   689→9.  identify any unallocated or unscheduled work.
   690→   690→10. Return a schedule with dates, topics, hours, and status.
   691→   691→
   692→   692→The implementation may use a greedy allocation algorithm for the first
   693→   693→version. The algorithm should be deterministic for the same inputs so
   694→   694→that results are repeatable and testable.
   695→   695→
   696→   696→### 5.7 High-Yield Mode
   697→   697→
   698→   698→High-Yield Mode shall activate when:
   699→   699→
   700→   700→\[ TotalEstimatedHours \> AvailableHours \]
   701→   701→
   702→   702→In this mode, the application shall: - Prefer topics with higher
   703→   703→priority. - Respect known prerequisites where possible. - Allocate
   704→   704→available time according to priority and estimated effort. - Mark topics
   705→   705→receiving reduced time. - Identify topics that could not be scheduled. -
   706→   706→Show a clear warning that the plan is compressed and may not cover every
   707→   707→topic adequately.
   708→   708→
   709→   709→The system shall never imply that a compressed plan guarantees
   710→   710→examination readiness.
   711→   711→
   712→   712→### 5.8 Schedule Quality Rules
   713→   713→
   714→   714→The generated plan should satisfy these rules:
   715→   715→
   716→   716→-   No task shall have a negative duration.
   717→   717→-   No daily allocation shall exceed the configured daily limit.
   718→   718→-   No task shall be scheduled after the exam date.
   719→   719→-   Every input topic shall be accounted for as scheduled, completed,
   720→   720→    deferred, or unscheduled.
   721→   721→-   Total assigned hours shall not exceed available hours.
   722→   722→-   Prerequisites should be scheduled before dependent topics when
   723→   723→    feasible.
   724→   724→-   Revision time shall not overlap with the examination date.
   725→   725→-   Rounding shall not cause the daily capacity to be exceeded.
   726→   726→

   810→   810→student laptop, excluding unusually slow file operations.

   812→   812→**NFR-02:** Dashboard interactions should respond without unnecessary
   813→   813→full-page delays.
   814→   814→
   815→   815→**NFR-03:** The application should avoid unnecessary large datasets and
   816→   816→heavy model dependencies.
   817→   817→
   818→   818→### 7.2 Usability
   819→   819→
   820→   820→**NFR-04:** A first-time user should be able to create a basic study
   821→   821→plan without technical assistance.
   822→   822→
   823→   823→**NFR-05:** Error messages shall explain the problem and suggest
   824→   824→corrective action.
   825→   825→
   826→   826→**NFR-06:** Complexity and priority scores shall be accompanied by brief
   827→   827→explanations.
   828→   828→
   829→   829→**NFR-07:** Charts shall include readable labels, legends, and tooltips
   830→   830→where supported.
   831→   831→
   832→   832→### 7.3 Reliability
   833→   833→
   834→   834→**NFR-08:** The system shall validate input data before running
   835→   835→calculations.
   836→   836→
   837→   837→**NFR-09:** Invalid or incomplete records shall not silently produce
   838→   838→misleading schedules.
   839→   839→
   840→   840→**NFR-10:** The same valid inputs and configuration shall produce the
   841→   841→same schedule.
   842→   842→
   843→   843→**NFR-11:** The application shall handle empty datasets, duplicate topic
   844→   844→IDs, and missing optional fields safely.
   845→   845→
   846→   846→### 7.4 Maintainability
   847→   847→
   848→   848→**NFR-12:** UI code, data handling, complexity calculations, and

   850→   850→

   852→   852→configuration values rather than scattered throughout the codebase.
   853→   853→

   855→   855→core scheduling algorithm.
   856→   856→

   858→   858→independently testable.
   859→   859→
   860→   860→### 7.5 Portability
   861→   861→

   863→   863→macOS environments with the required Python packages installed.
   864→   864→

   866→   866→
   867→   867→### 7.6 Security and Privacy
   868→   868→
   869→   869→**NFR-18:** The initial version shall not request unnecessary personal
   870→   870→information.
   871→   871→
   872→   872→**NFR-19:** Uploaded CSV files shall be validated before processing.
   873→   873→
   874→   874→**NFR-20:** The application shall not execute uploaded file contents as

   876→   876→
   877→   877→**NFR-21:** If plans are stored locally, the application shall explain
   878→   878→where the data is stored and how it can be reset.
   879→   879→
   880→   880→**NFR-22:** If deployed online in a future version, authentication,

   897→   897→
   898→   898→The application shall handle at least the following cases:
   899→   899→
   900→   900→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   901→   901→  Condition                           Expected system response
   902→   902→  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------

   904→   904→                                      a valid future date

   907→   907→

   915→   915→                                      IDs

   920→   920→                                      explain the allowed range
   921→   921→

  1060→  1060→## 12. Constraints and Limitations
  1061→  1061→
  1062→  1062→1.  Topic complexity is estimated from manually assigned attributes and
  1063→  1063→    is not an objective measurement of a student's ability.

  1066→  1066→    reliable student-performance data.
  1067→  1067→4.  Preparation-time estimates are guidance values and may differ from
  1068→  1068→    actual study time.
  1069→  1069→5.  The initial version uses a rule-based model. It should not be
  1070→  1070→    described as a trained machine-learning model unless training data,
  1071→  1071→    model evaluation, and a genuine training process are implemented.
  1072→  1072→6.  High-Yield Mode helps prioritize limited time but cannot guarantee
  1073→  1073→    complete syllabus coverage or examination success.
  1074→  1074→7.  Automatic syllabus extraction and external syllabus synchronization
  1075→  1075→    are not part of the initial release.
  1076→  1076→8.  The quality of the schedule depends on the quality of the supplied
  1077→  1077→    topic data and student inputs.
